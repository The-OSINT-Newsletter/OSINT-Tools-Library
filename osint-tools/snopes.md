### What does Snopes do?

Snopes investigates claims circulating online and publishes assessments supported by evidence and source material.

Its coverage includes:

* Viral social-media claims.
* Misleading or manipulated images.
* Videos and memes.
* Political claims.
* Rumours and hoaxes.
* Misleading statistics.
* Conspiracy theories.
* Satire presented as fact.
* AI-generated or manipulated content.

The platform uses ratings including False, Mostly False, Mixture, Mostly True, True and Unproven, although it increasingly publishes investigations and explainers that do not necessarily receive a simple true/false rating.

**The lowdown:** Plain and simple it’s a verification tool. It also maintains a large searchable archive, making it useful for checking whether a particular rumour or image has previously been investigated.&#x20;

### Cookies:&#x20;

The site uses first- and third-party cookies for functionality, referral attribution, advertising and audience measurement. Observed cookies included Criteo's cto\_bundle, Prebid Unified ID cookies (pbjs-unifiedid and pbjs-unifiedid\_cst), Scorecard Research identifiers (UID and XID), a first-party referral cookie (snopes\_tdata) and a regional user-location cookie.

### Use in Reporting

Snopes can be used to:

* Verify claims encountered during an OSINT investigation.
* Identify misinformation, hoaxes and manipulated narratives circulating online.
* Determine whether an image, video, quotation or social-media post has previously been investigated.
* Corroborate findings from social-media, reverse-image and open-web research.
* Identify the original sources and evidence used to assess a disputed claim.
* Document fact-check ratings, publication dates and supporting evidence for an investigative record.
* Identify the origins and development of recurring rumours or misinformation narratives.

| **Capabilities**                                                                                  | **Limitations**                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provides researched assessments of online claims and rumours.                                     | Snopes is a secondary source, not the original evidence behind every claim.                                                       |
| Covers images, videos, social-media posts, quotations, statistics and narratives.                 | A fact-check may not cover a new variation of an existing rumour.                                                                 |
| Provides explanations and supporting sources rather than simply labelling content true or false.  | Some claims are genuinely inconclusive. Snopes uses ‘Unproven’ where available evidence does not support a definitive conclusion  |

| Can help identify misinformation, satire, manipulated content and misleading context.             | The quality of a conclusion depends partly on the evidence available to the researchers at the time.                              |

### Summary

Snopes itself has increasingly described its work as extending beyond traditional fact-checks into deeper news explainers and investigations, particularly where claims cannot be reduced neatly to ‘true’ or ‘false’.&#x20;

The platform is particularly valuable during the verification stage of the OSINT workflow when an investigator encounters a viral claim, questionable photograph, suspicious quotation, manipulated image or piece of misinformation.

### Ownership

The site is owned by [Chris Richmond ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Richmond_\(entrepreneur\))and Drew Schoentrup, who acquired 100% of the company in September 2022. It was originally owned and founded by David and Barbara Mikkelson who launched the site in 1994 as an urban legend and folklore reference resource.

### Ethical Considerations

* Follow and assess the underlying sources cited by Snopes.
* Be aware that some claims are genuinely inconclusive and may receive an Unproven assessment.
* Check publication dates because misinformation can reappear in altered forms years later.
* Avoid presenting a Snopes rating without explaining what specific version of the claim was assessed.
* Consider whether the original material has subsequently changed or been deleted.
* Do not assume that because Snopes has investigated a claim, every related claim or variation has also been investigated.
