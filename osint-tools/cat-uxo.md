### What does CAT UXO do?

CAT UXO (Collective Awareness to Unexploded Ordnance) brings together information from open sources and contributors to create a global database of explosive hazards. Its searchable categories include landmines, submunitions, grenades, aircraft bombs, rockets, missiles, mortars, projectiles, fuzes, naval devices, SALW, UCAVs, AFVs and IEDs, as well as incidents and terrorist-group information.

The stated purpose is to improve awareness of explosive hazards and reduce the risk of injury or death for people working in or travelling through affected areas.

**The lowdown:** It’s most useful as a specialist reference and verification source when an investigation involves an unidentified munition, suspected UXO, mine, explosive device or historic explosive-hazard incident.&#x20;

**Note:** CAT UXO explicitly warns that its database is not exhaustive or fully accurate. It should therefore be treated as supporting intelligence rather than definitive technical identification.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

CAT UXO can be used to:

* Identify and contextualise suspected UXO, mines, munitions and other explosive hazards.
* Compare an unidentified object against documented explosive-hazard examples.
* Research historical or reported explosive-hazard incidents.
* Corroborate findings from imagery, military, governmental and specialist EOD sources.
* Document relevant terminology, classifications, photographs and database references.
* Support investigations involving conflict zones, military activity, historic battlefields or suspected explosive remnants.
* Provide supporting context when assessing reports or imagery involving explosive hazards.

As a real-world example, [an ICJ document in proceedings re Ukraine v Russia](https://icj-web.leman.un-icc.cloud/sites/default/files/case-related/166/166-20230606-ora-01-00-bi.pdf) cites CAT UXO's “SPM Limpet Mine” entry as a source alongside Ukrainian forensic evidence and witness testimony concerning a limpet mine discovered in the Kharkiv region. The CAT-UXO page was specifically listed in the judges' materials.&#x20;

### Summary

CAT UXO fits best during the discovery, enrichment, analysis and verification stages of the OSINT workflow. Investigators can use it after identifying a suspected munition, explosive hazard, UXO, mine or related incident to establish the identity, characteristics and wider context of the item.

It’s particularly useful for image-based investigations involving military equipment, conflict zones and unexploded ordnance.

### Ethical Considerations

* Treat CAT UXO entries as indicators, not definitive identification.
* Do not approach, move, handle or interfere with suspected explosive devices.
* Verify significant identifications using authoritative or qualified EOD sources.
* Avoid publishing precise information that could unnecessarily expose people to explosive hazards.
* Do not use CAT-UXO information as a substitute for authorised EOD procedures.
