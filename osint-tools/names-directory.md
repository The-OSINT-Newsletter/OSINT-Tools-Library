### What does Names Directory do?

Names Directory provides searchable associations between first names and surnames, including variations, misspellings, and similar-name information. The site states that its data is collected by crawlers from public databases or websites and that the numbers shown beside names indicate how often a particular combination was found.

**The lowdown:** It’s essentially a name-association and name-variation discovery tool that can help generate leads when researching an individual, username, or unfamiliar name.

### Cookies:&#x20;

The site uses Google advertising and consent-management cookies, including \_\_gads, \_\_gpi, \_\_eoi, FCCDCF and FCNEC. These are primarily associated with advertising, measurement and privacy/consent management rather than the core name-search function.

### Use in Reporting

Names Directory can be used to:

* Identify possible name variations, spellings and naming patterns.
* Generate potential leads when researching an individual's name or surname.
* Establish whether a particular name appears to be associated with a particular country or region.
* Provide background context when assessing the likely origin or distribution of a name.
* Cross-reference names against other open-source records and databases.
* Support identity-resolution research by generating additional search terms or variants.

As an example, a researcher could use Names Directory to investigate an uncommon surname, identify its apparent geographic distribution, and then use those locations and spelling variants as search terms across authoritative sources such as company registers, electoral records, court records, news archives or professional databases.

**Reporting Caveat:** Names Directory should be treated as a lead-generation and corroboration tool, rather than definitive evidence of a person's identity, location, or relationship to another individual.&#x20;

### Summary

Names Directory is a free, lightweight name-association tool that’s most useful during the discovery and lead-generation stages of an OSINT investigation. It can reveal possible first-name/surname combinations and spelling variants, but results should be treated as leads rather than verified identity evidence.

### Ownership

Ownership details are not publicly disclosed. The site identifies itself as having started in 2007.

### Ethical Considerations

* Treat results as leads rather than verified facts; a name match does not establish identity.
* Be aware that multiple people may share the same name, creating a risk of misidentification.
* Avoid using name information to make unsupported assumptions about a person's identity, background or location.
* Record the date accessed and source URL, as directory information may change or become outdated.
* Clearly distinguish between information found through Names Directory and independently verified information.
