### What does SunCalc do?

SunCalc calculates the position of the sun throughout the day for any location on Earth. It displays the sun's azimuth, elevation, sunrise and sunset times, golden hour, twilight periods and the direction that shadows should fall at a given date and time.

It’s commonly used to verify images and videos by comparing observed shadows and lighting conditions with the predicted solar position.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** The tool can help investigators estimate when media was captured, validate a claimed location, or identify inconsistencies that may indicate manipulated or misleading content.

**Note:** Although SunCalc provides highly accurate astronomical calculations, it should always be used alongside other geolocation techniques, as shadows can be affected by terrain, weather, camera angle and surrounding structures.

### Use in Reporting

SunCalc can be used to:&#x20;

* Demonstrate whether shadow direction matches the claimed time and location.
* Support geolocation by validating the orientation of buildings, roads and landmarks.
* Verify or challenge the claimed capture time of photographs and videos.

The tool is widely used in OSINT to verify the timing, location and authenticity of photographs and videos. For example, [Bellingcat's investigation](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/uk-and-europe/2014/07/28/two-more-key-sightings-of-the-mh17-buk-missile-launcher/) into the MH17 Buk missile launcher, used SunCalc’s shadow analysis to estimate image capture times.

### Summary

SunCalc is most valuable during the analysis and validation phases of the OSINT workflow, where investigators need to assess whether lighting conditions, shadow direction and solar position are consistent with the claimed location and time of an image or video.&#x20;

It provides strong supporting evidence but should always be used alongside other geolocation and verification techniques.

### Ethical Considerations

* Use SunCalc only to verify publicly available imagery and lawful evidence.
* Avoid presenting solar analysis as conclusive proof without corroboration.
* Document all assumptions and calculation inputs.
* Verify findings using multiple independent sources where possible.
* Respect privacy and applicable legislation when investigating individuals or locations.
