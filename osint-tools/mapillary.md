### What does Mapillary do?

Mapillary provides access to crowdsourced street-level imagery that helps users visually inspect real-world locations and verify geographic information.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** It’s essentially a community-driven alternative to Street View, combining user photos with AI mapping tools.<br>

### Use in Reporting

Mapillary is useful for:

* Verifying location of images/videos.
* Supporting conflict and disaster reporting.
* Tracking infrastructure or environmental change.
* Fact-checking visual claims in journalism.
* Assisting OpenStreetMap updates.

You can view this [case study](https://www.casestudies.com/company/mapillary/case-study/how-red-cross-uses-data-during-global-disasters) re Mapillary’s use during disaster response.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                      | **Limitations**                                                    |
| ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Global street-level image browsing.   | Coverage varies heavily by region. Some areas have little/no data. |
| Geolocation and visual verification.  | Not real-time. Some images may be outdated.                        |
| Historical comparison of locations.   | Privacy filters reduce image detail.                               |
| AI-based object and road detection.   | Less comprehensive than Google Street View.                        |
| API integration of mapping workflows. | <p><br></p>                                                        |

### Summary

Mapillary sits fairly early on in a typical OSINT workflow, mainly as a geolocation and visual verification layer rather than a starting point or final proof source.

### Ownership

Owned by Meta Platforms, Inc. (Facebook parent company), following its acquisition of the Swedish startup Mapillary AB in 2020.

### Ethical Considerations

* Potential privacy concerns (faces, vehicles, private property visibility).
* Must be used responsibly in OSINT to avoid doxxing or identifying private individuals.
* Some imagery may be user-submitted and contextually misleading.
* Ethical OSINT practice requires verification across multiple sources.
