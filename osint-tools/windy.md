### What does Windy do?

Windy provides an interactive map for visualising weather conditions and forecasts around the world. Users can select a location, view different meteorological layers, move through forecast times and compare available forecast models.

Its capabilities include wind visualisation, temperature and precipitation, radar and satellite, forecast model comparisons, location-specific forecasts, additional environmental layers, webcams and weather stations, and forecast history and archives.

**The lowdown:** It’s particularly useful for adding environmental context to an investigation.

**Important note:** Windy does not independently establish that an event occurred, identify the people involved, or prove the location of a photograph or video. Forecasts are model-derived estimates, and the available imagery, resolution, update frequency and historical coverage vary by layer and subscription.

### Use in Reporting

Windy can be used to:

* Examine forecast weather conditions around a reported event location and time.
* Provide environmental context for reporting on storms, floods, wildfires and other natural hazards.
* Assess wind and wave conditions relevant to maritime incidents or vessel movements.
* Review weather conditions that may affect aviation, outdoor operations or emergency response.
* Compare forecast models to identify areas of agreement or uncertainty.
* Examine available radar and satellite imagery to understand the development and movement of weather systems.
* Compare reported weather conditions with meteorological information when checking photographs, videos or eyewitness accounts.
* Generate leads for further investigation using official weather services, archived imagery and other independent sources.
* Document the forecast conditions that were available at a particular time, including the selected layer, model and timeline.
* Provide background context for investigations involving transport disruption, public events, search-and-rescue activity or extreme weather.

As an example of its use and limitations, [The NTSB investigated the capsizing and sinking of a commercial fishing vessel off Alaska](https://npfvoa.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/MAR2102.pdf) on 31 December 2019, in which five of the seven crew members were lost. Investigators documented the captain's use of Windy and interviewed other fishing captains who used it to check forecasts while in port and at sea.

It was found that Windy's visual weather layers did not provide the same detailed text-based warnings as the US National Weather Service, including urgent warnings about heavy freezing spray. The NTSB considered whether reliance on Windy could have contributed to important information being missed.

### Summary

Windy fits best in the collection and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow, particularly when an investigation involves weather, maritime activity, aviation, natural hazards or the verification of location- and time-specific claims. I

Its principal value in investigative reporting is contextual and corroborative rather than conclusive. Important findings should be checked against official meteorological records, original imagery and other independent evidence, with the forecast model, timestamp and limitations clearly documented.

### Ethical Considerations

* Distinguish forecast data from observations and satellite or radar imagery.
* Be cautious when using weather conditions to geolocate photographs or videos, as similar conditions can occur across large areas.
* Do not infer a person's presence or movements from weather data alone.
* Avoid publishing unsupported conclusions about the cause of an incident based solely on a weather visualisation.
* Check the terms of use before reproducing maps or imagery in a report. Windy's terms permit certain uses of screenshots, videos and animated GIFs, but exclude radar-map visualisations and webcam images from the general visualisation permission.
