### What does Bitbo do?

Bitbo is a Bitcoin data and analytics platform providing live and historical information on Bitcoin price, market activity, mining, supply and on-chain metrics.

The platform provides more than 150 Bitcoin charts, including metrics covering realised price, mining difficulty, hashrate, supply, market cycles and holder behaviour. It also provides a Bitcoin data API for programmatic access to on-chain metrics and technical indicators.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** It provides market and on-chain context around Bitcoin activity.

### Use in Reporting

Bitbo can be used to:

* Research Bitcoin price, market, mining and on-chain activity at a specific point in time.
* Investigate historical Bitcoin metrics relevant to cryptocurrency-related activity.
* Identify patterns and trends in Bitcoin supply, holder behaviour, mining and market cycles.
* Corroborate findings from blockchain explorers and specialist cryptocurrency-intelligence platforms.
* Document Bitcoin metrics, chart values, dates, timestamps and source URLs as supporting evidence.
* Provide market and blockchain context when assessing cryptocurrency transactions or incidents.
* Support financial-crime, cryptocurrency and cryptocurrency-related OSINT investigations.

Bitbo data has been cited in published cryptocurrency and financial research, including [GoMining's Q4 2025 institutional report. ](https://institutional.gomining.com/docs/institutional-gomining_q4-2025-report.pdf)

### Summary

Bitbo fits best during the enrichment, analysis, and verification stages of the OSINT workflow. Investigators can use it after identifying a Bitcoin transaction, address, incident or relevant date to establish the wider Bitcoin-market and blockchain context.&#x20;

However, it’s not a substitute for a blockchain explorer or specialist blockchain-intelligence platform.

### Ownership

Bitbo was founded in 2020 by Christopher Gimmer and Marc Chouinard, headquartered in Ontario, Canada.

### Ethical Considerations

* Use publicly available blockchain information for legitimate and proportionate investigative purposes.
* Do not treat a Bitcoin address as automatically identifying a real-world individual or organisation.
* Corroborate significant findings with the underlying blockchain and independent sources.
* Record the date/time of data collection because market and on-chain metrics can change.
* Distinguish clearly between raw blockchain data and analytical metrics or interpretations.
* Avoid presenting market indicators or predictions as established facts.
* Be aware that cryptocurrency data can contribute to the identification or profiling of individuals when combined with other information.
