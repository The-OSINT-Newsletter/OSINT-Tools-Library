### What does USPTO do?

USPTO is the official US government platform for patents and trademarks, allowing you to spot companies before they go public with products, uncover who owns a brand, and trace links between businesses and individuals through filings.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** It’s an OSINT goldmine for uncovering who owns patents and trademarks, tracking innovation, and mapping corporate connections.

### Use in Reporting

USPTO records are useful for:

* Confirming ownership of brands and logos.
* Revealing early-stage business activity.
* Adding credible, official documentation to investigations.
* Strengthening corporate intelligence and attribution.

USPTO records are often cited in journalism, legal reporting, and OSINT investigations to verify ownership, track innovation, and uncover corporate connections. For example, the long-running dispute between NTP (a patent holding company) and Blackberry maker Research in Motion (RIM) [was covered in legal and tech reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTP%2C_Inc) with journalists referencing USPTO patent holdings and reexamination activity.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                 | **Limitations**                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ |

### Summary

USPTO is a goldmine for corporate and innovation OSINT, best used in the collection and enrichment stage of the OSINT workflow to gather verified, official data on companies, individuals, and intellectual property, and to add depth to existing findings.

### Ownership

United States Government.

### Ethical Considerations

* Handle personal details responsibly.
* Avoid misinterpreting inactive or abandoned filings.
* Use filings as supporting evidence, not sole proof.
* Be mindful of context when linking individuals to companies.
