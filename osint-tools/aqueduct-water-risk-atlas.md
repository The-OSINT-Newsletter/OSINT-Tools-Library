### What does Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas do?

Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas is essentially a global water-risk screening tool. It translates complex hydrological data into accessible indicators showing where water-related risks are concentrated.

The current Aqueduct 4.0 framework includes indicators covering water quantity, quality and other water-related risks, alongside future projections for selected indicators based on climate projections.

It can be used to investigate whether a location, company operation, agricultural area, infrastructure project or community is exposed to significant water-related risk.

**The lowdown:** It’s particularly useful for environmental, climate, corporate accountability and infrastructure investigations where water availability or water-related hazards are relevant.<br>

### Use in Reporting

Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas can be used to:

* Assess water risk at specific locations.
* Identify areas exposed to water stress or scarcity.
* Contextualise environmental and climate-related investigations.
* Compare water risk across sites, regions or assets.
* Support reporting on corporate and infrastructure exposure.
* Corroborate claims about local water-related pressures.

As a real-world example, [WRI mapped Owens Corning's facilities](https://www.wri.org/research/case-study-aqueduct-informs-owens-corning-corporate-water-strategy) (a multinational building materials manufacturer) against current and projected water stress to identify which operations faced higher water-supply risks.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                                          | **Limitations**                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Maps water risk across locations worldwide using standardised indicators. | Results are modelled estimates rather than direct measurements.        |
| Assesses risks including water stress, depletion, drought, and flooding.  | Baseline data is not real-time and may not reflect current conditions. |
| Compares water risk between different locations, sites, and regions.      | Future projections are scenarios rather than precise predictions.      |
| Provides historical baseline data and future risk projections.            | Global datasets may overlook important local or site-specific factors. |
| Allows results and datasets to be exported for further analysis.          | Data quality and accuracy depend on the underlying models and inputs.  |
| Supports GIS analysis using downloadable spatial data.                    | <p><br></p>                                                            |

### Summary

Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas fits best during environmental context, verification and risk assessment, helping investigators establish whether a location is exposed to water stress, depletion, drought, flooding or other water-related pressures.

However, it should be treated as a screening and contextualisation tool rather than standalone proof. Important findings should be cross-checked against local data, government records, scientific research, satellite imagery, field evidence or other independent sources.

### Ownership

Aqueduct is developed and maintained by the World Resources Institute (WRI), a global research organisation focused on environmental and sustainability issues. It is co-developed and guided collaboratively with the Aqueduct Alliance, which includes corporate partners (such as Bloomberg, GE, and Shell) alongside government agencies and foundations.

### Ethical Considerations

* Verify modelled findings against independent local evidence.
* Do not present risk scores as proof of an environmental incident.
* Clearly distinguish historical data from future projections.
* Explain uncertainty when using modelled or aggregated results.
* Avoid overstating risks to communities or individual locations.
* Preserve dataset versions and methodology when citing results.
