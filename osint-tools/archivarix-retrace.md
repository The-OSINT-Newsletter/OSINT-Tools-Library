### What does Archivarix Retrace do?

Archivarix Retrace takes a photo, a GIF or a short video and builds a report about where it came from. It searches the open web for the same picture and lists earlier copies together with the pages that carry them, and gives the earliest date the image can be traced to.&#x20;

It reads text, signs and landmarks in the frame and the metadata the file still carries (EXIF, and GPS when present), and marks the place on a map, with the precision of the estimate, when the frame or its copies make it possible to name it.

Every claim in the report links to the page it rests on, and each finding shows how strong it is; when nothing reliable is found, the report says so. People are identified only through open sources: when pages that carry the same picture name who is in it, the report shows that name with its link. There is no face recognition.

**The lowdown:** It helps establish the provenance and context of photographs, GIFs and short videos.

### Use in Reporting

Archivarix Retrace is useful for:

* Checking whether a photo presented as new is old or recycled: the earliest copy and its date show when it first appeared.
* Finding the original publisher or photographer to credit or contact.
* Locating where a photo was taken from text, signs and landmarks in the frame, or from captions on earlier copies.
* Finding where your own photo is reused without permission.

**Example:** For the well-known Eiffel Tower photo from Wikimedia Commons, Retrace found 30+ exact copies, placed the shot on the Champ de Mars and showed it was online no later than February 2008: [https://archivarix.com/en/blog/retrace/](https://archivarix.com/en/blog/retrace/)&#x20;

### Summary

Archivarix Retrace is an image and video provenance tool that searches publicly accessible webpages and archives for earlier copies of submitted visual material. Its most useful at the verification stage: after an image surfaces and before it is reported or reused, to establish its origin, age and location.

**Note:** Its evidence-linked reports make it useful for investigative journalism and research, but findings must be independently checked.

### Ownership

Retrace is built and run by Archivarix ([https://archivarix.com/](https://archivarix.com/)) (and founded by David Archivarix,) which has run a service for restoring websites from the Wayback Machine since 2017 and also makes Archivarix Tube Search (archived and deleted YouTube videos) and Web Archive Echo. One Archivarix account works across all three tools.

### Ethical Considerations

* Use it to check images, not to profile, track or expose people; the terms of service forbid stalking, harassment, doxxing and building dossiers on individuals.
* Names in a report come only from open pages that carry the same picture; verify them before publishing and weigh the person's privacy.
* A report is evidence to weigh, not a verdict; confirm key findings independently before reporting.
* Uploading shares the file with a third-party service; do not upload material you are not allowed to share.
* Take extra care with images of minors and victims, and with publishing where a photo was taken, as that can put people at risk.
