### What does Deepware do?

Deepware applies multiple detection models to uploaded media, looking for artefacts commonly associated with deepfake generation techniques such as facial synthesis, face swapping, lip synchronisation and AI-generated speech.

Results are presented as a confidence assessment rather than a definitive conclusion. (As deepfake generation techniques evolve rapidly, no automated detection tool can guarantee complete accuracy.)

**The lowdown:** It’s best used as an initial screening tool within a wider verification process.

### Use in Reporting

Deepware can be used to:

* Screen suspected deepfake videos before publication.
* Support verification during breaking news and crisis events.
* Assess user-generated content circulating on social media.
* Corroborate findings during misinformation and disinformation investigations.
* Assist journalists and investigators when authenticating visual evidence.
* Provide an initial forensic assessment prior to expert analysis.

Deepfake detection tools such as deepware have become part of verification workflows used by journalists, fact-checkers and OSINT investigators when analysing manipulated media relating to elections, armed conflicts, financial fraud and misinformation campaigns.

### Summary

Deepware is most valuable after a video or audio file has been identified and before it’s relied upon as evidence/included in reporting. It provides an initial assessment of whether media may contain AI-generated or manipulated content, helping investigators prioritise material for further forensic examination.

### Ownership

According to Tracxn, Deepware is an unfunded company based in Sarajevo (Bosnia And Herzegovina), founded in 2018 by [Akif Iris](https://platform.tracxn.com/a/d/people/58b3aafde4b072d103006493/akifiris) and [Orhan Akyurek](https://platform.tracxn.com/a/d/people/58b3aa4ce4b072d103006356/orhanakyurek). Its parent company is Zemana who researched methods to develop an AI-based antivirus engine.

### Ethical Considerations

* Detection scores should not be presented as definitive evidence that media is fake.
* Avoid making accusations based solely on automated AI detection results.
* Corroborate findings through metadata, provenance, contextual analysis and independent verification.
* Consider the possibility of false positives and false negatives, particularly with compressed or low-quality media.
* Be transparent about the limitations of AI detection when reporting findings.
