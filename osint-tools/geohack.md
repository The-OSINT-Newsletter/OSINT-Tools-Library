### What does Geohack do?

GeoHack takes a set of geographic coordinates and provides a central page of links to different mapping services and geographic information resources. Rather than having to enter the same coordinates into several websites individually, you can use GeoHack to access multiple geographic references from one location.

It’s particularly useful when investigating locations identified through photographs, videos, satellite imagery, public records, news reports or other OSINT sources. It can also display coordinate information in different formats and provide additional geographic references, including UTM and national grid information.

**The lowdown:** The tool is primarily a gateway to other mapping services rather than a mapping platform in its own right, helping users compare the same point using different maps and geographic resources.

### How to Use:

**1. Open the** [**GeoHack website**](https://geohack.toolforge.org/) **or alternatively, open a GeoHack link from a Wikipedia article containing geographic coordinates. Then enter the latitude and longitude in a supported coordinate format. E.g. Latitude: 51.5007, Longitude -0.1246**

### Use in Reporting

GeoHack is useful for:

* Checking and cross-referencing latitude and longitude coordinates obtained during an investigation.
* Opening the same geographic location in multiple mapping services.
* Supporting geolocation research involving photographs, videos, satellite imagery and publicly available information.
* Checking whether coordinates supplied in a report, article or public record correspond to the stated location.
* Converting between geographic coordinate formats, including decimal degrees and degrees, minutes and seconds.
* Accessing additional geographic references, including UTM and national grid information where supported.
* Providing a convenient starting point for further mapping and geographic analysis.
* Helping researchers document and compare geographic reference points used in an investigation.

| **Capabilities**                                                                | **Limitations**                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provides links to multiple mapping services for a single coordinate location.   | Does not independently identify a location from an image or video.                           |
| Supports decimal-degree and degrees/minutes/seconds coordinate formats.         | Depends on the accuracy of the coordinates supplied by the user or source.                   |
| Provides access to additional geographic references, including UTM information. | Does not replace specialist GIS software or detailed geospatial analysis.                    |
| Supports geographic parameters such as feature type, region and map scale.      | External mapping services may have different coverage, imagery dates and levels of detail.   |
| Can be used without an account or subscription.                                 | Does not verify the authenticity, date or provenance of the geographic information supplied. |

### Summary

GeoHack is particularly useful as part of a wider geolocation workflow, helping researchers move from a known coordinate to more detailed mapping, satellite imagery and geographic investigation.

However, it’s not a standalone geolocation or verification tool. Any conclusions drawn from the maps and geographic resources it links to should be checked against original imagery, reliable geographic data and independent evidence.

### Ownership

GeoHack is hosted on Wikimedia Toolforge, the Wikimedia community's platform for hosting tools and applications. It’s a community-maintained project associated with the Wikimedia ecosystem rather than a commercial mapping service.&#x20;

### Ethical Considerations

* Verify coordinates against reliable sources before using them in reporting.
* Do not assume that a coordinate proves an individual was present at a particular location.
* Be careful when publishing precise coordinates for private residences, vulnerable people, endangered species or sensitive facilities.
* Consider whether the location information could expose individuals to harassment, unwanted attention or physical risk.
* Remember that external mapping services may use different imagery dates, geographic datasets and levels of accuracy.
* Do not treat a map link or a location marker as independent proof of an investigative claim.
