### What does The World Bank Open Data Catalog do?

The World Bank Open Data Catalog is a central discovery platform for development data, allowing you to search and filter datasets by areas such as country/economy, source catalogue, collection, resource type and licence. Dataset pages provide metadata and resources, while some datasets offer APIs for programmatic access.

It can therefore be particularly useful in OSINT when you need reliable contextual or statistical information about a country, region, population, economy or development issue.

**The lowdown:** It’s a large, authoritative development-data repository that’s particularly useful for adding statistical and socioeconomic context to an OSINT investigation and corroborating claims with structured data.

### How to Use:

**1. Search the catalogue for a subject, country, organisation, indicator or dataset relevant to your investigation.**

### Use in Reporting

The World Bank Open Data Catalog can be used to:

* Obtain official development and socioeconomic statistics for countries and economies.
* Establish statistical context around an investigation.
* Compare countries, regions or time periods using structured datasets.
* Corroborate claims relating to population, economics, health, education, infrastructure, finance and development.
* Identify datasets and indicators that can generate further investigative leads.
* Download machine-readable data for independent analysis.
* Cross-reference World Bank data with government, academic and other authoritative sources.

As an example, an IDS working paper on Mozambique cited both [World Bank Open Data](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5e59257ee90e071111640425/Wp523_Online.pdf) and specific World Bank indicators in its research. A report examining primary healthcare in Europe also used [World Bank Open Data](https://medicosypacientes.cgcom.es/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/mocha-issues-and-opportunities-in-primary-health-care-for-children-in-europe-1.pdf) to investigate population, mortality, fertility and other health indicators.&#x20;

### Summary

The World Bank Open Data Catalog is a valuable OSINT source for finding authoritative development, economic, and socioeconomic datasets that can provide context, corroborate claims, and support data-driven analysis. It’s best used to verify and corroborate claims with authoritative statistical data, although the data can also support your analysis.&#x20;

### Ownership

The catalogue is operated by the World Bank Group, but the World Bank explicitly notes that not every dataset in the catalogue is owned by the World Bank Group. Ownership and licensing are specified in individual dataset metadata.

### Ethical Considerations

* Don't assume every catalogue entry has identical reuse conditions.
* Document the exact dataset, source and date accessed.
* Make sure you understand how the statistic was collected and what it actually measures.
* Beware of outdated data.
* Don't overinterpret statistics: correlation does not establish causation.
* Some microdata can contain information relating to individuals and have additional restrictions.
* Attribute correctly: World Bank Dataset Terms generally require attribution for World Bank datasets.
