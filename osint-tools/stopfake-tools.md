### What does StopFake Tools do?

StopFake Tools investigates questionable claims and media narratives, particularly those relating to Ukraine and Russian disinformation, and publishes fact-checks and analytical material. Its stated purpose is to check facts, verify information, and refute distorted information and propaganda.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** It’s ideal for checking whether a specific claim, article, image or news story has already been fact-checked.&#x20;

**Note:** StopFake itself maintains a corrections policy and acknowledges that fact-checking assessments can be challenged where new evidence is provided.

### Use in Reporting

StopFake can be used to:

* Check whether a particular claim or narrative has already been fact-checked.
* Identify evidence used to challenge misinformation or disinformation.
* Corroborate findings from independent OSINT and media sources.
* Establish the history and context of a disputed claim or narrative.
* Document relevant fact-checks, publication dates and supporting evidence.
* Support reporting on misinformation, propaganda and information operations.
* Help distinguish between established evidence and unsupported or misleading claims.

The Canadian Security Intelligence Service (CSIS) in its publication ‘[Who Said What? The Security Challenges of Modern Disinformation’](https://www.canada.ca/en/security-intelligence-service/corporate/publications/who-said-what-the-security-challenges-of-modern-disinformation/chapter-9-countering-disinformation-in-ukraine.html) has a chapter on countering disinformation in Ukraine in which it discusses StopFake as a fact-checking project and source of research on Russian disinformation.&#x20;

| **Capabilities**                                             | **Limitations**                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Provides published fact-checks and investigative analysis.   | Coverage is selective - not every claim will have been investigated.                          |
| Can provide evidence and sources supporting a fact-check.    | Findings should not automatically be treated as definitive proof.                             |
| Useful for identifying recurring misinformation narratives.  | Subject matter and geographic coverage can be uneven.                                         |
| Can provide historical context around disputed claims.       | Some investigations may rely on information that later changes.                               |
| Useful for corroborating independent OSINT findings.         | Researchers should examine the underlying evidence rather than relying solely on the verdict. |

### Summary

StopFake fits best during the verification and analysis stages of the OSINT workflow. It’s particularly useful for misinformation and disinformation investigations and can be used after identifying a questionable claim, article, image or narrative to check whether it has already been investigated and to obtain additional evidence and context.

### Ownership

Owned and operated by StopFake, a Ukrainian fact-checking organisation associated with the Mohyla School of Journalism. Its founding team included journalists, academics and students, with Yevhen Fedchenko identified as a co-founder and leader.&#x20;

### Ethical Considerations

* Treat StopFake assessments as evidence to evaluate, not automatically as definitive proof.
* Review the underlying sources and evidence supporting a fact-check.
* Consider potential bias, methodology and scope when assessing findings.
* Avoid presenting allegations or disputed claims as established facts.
* Clearly distinguish between StopFake's assessment and your own independently verified findings.
