### What does Maritime Database do?

Maritime Database provides maritime information, vessel tracking and port data, with a database covering 50,000+ vessels, 2,000+ ports and 150+ countries. Current position and port-call history are received through AIS (Automatic Identification System) and it also contains a substantial shipping-company directory, including companies categorised as ship owners, managers and operators.

**The lowdown:** It’s a global maritime OSINT and vessel-information resource combining vessel records, AIS-derived information, port data and maritime-company information.&#x20;

**Note:** Maritime Database itself warns that its AIS-derived and other vessel information is provided for informational purposes without assurance of accuracy or reliability.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

Maritime Database can be used to:

* Identify vessels using names, IMO numbers, MMSIs or callsigns.
* Investigate vessel specifications, flags, operators, management information and maritime affiliations.
* Research vessel movements, reported destinations, current ports and AIS-derived positions.
* Investigate relationships between vessels, shipping companies, ports and operators.
* Corroborate findings from AIS platforms, vessel registries, company records and other maritime OSINT sources.
* Document vessel identifiers, locations, voyage information, timestamps and source URLs as supporting evidence.
* Support investigations involving shipping activity, maritime security, sanctions research, smuggling or other maritime-related activity.

Maritime Database data was also used by the [American Alliance for Manufacturing](https://www.americanmanufacturing.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/AAM-Comment-re-Proposed-Modification-Section-301-Shipbuilding-07-07-2025.pdf) in a 2025 analysis of proposed U.S. shipbuilding fees covering 60 vessels. The report explicitly identifies Maritime Database as the source of vessel data used in its calculation.

### Summary

Maritime Database is particularly useful for establishing the identity and background of a vessel and developing links between vessels, identifiers, ports and maritime organisations. It fits well in the identification, enrichment and verification stages of the OSINT workflow, however, important claims should be independently corroborated before being presented as established fact.

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not assume that an AIS-derived position proves the vessel's exact physical location at a particular moment.
* Corroborate important vessel movements using independent AIS or official maritime sources.
* Distinguish between registered owner, beneficial owner, manager, operator and charterer; these are not necessarily the same entity.
* Be cautious when combining vessel information with personal information about crew, owners or private individuals.
* Consider sanctions, financial-crime and maritime-security implications when researching vessels or companies.
* Avoid publishing sensitive real-time information where doing so could create unnecessary safety or security risks.
