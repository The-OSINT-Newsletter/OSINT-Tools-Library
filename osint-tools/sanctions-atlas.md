### What does Sanctions Atlas do?

Sanctions Atlas brings together official sanctions data from OFAC, the UK, the EU, the UN, Canada, and Australia in one public research platform.

Users can search sanctioned people, organizations, and vessels; examine sanctions programs and authority coverage; review aliases, identifiers, designation histories, relationships, modifications, and delistings; and explore broader patterns through the analytical dashboard, global sanctions map, country and program pages, and sanctions changes feed.

Official-source data is refreshed multiple times daily. Each authority listing retains its source, official identifier, and provenance, and important findings can be checked through links to the issuing authority.

**The lowdown:** It saves time by bringing sanctions and ownership intelligence together in one platform.

### Use in Reporting

Sanctions Atlas can support reporting and investigations involving sanctioned people, organizations, companies, vessels, sanctions programs, geographic exposure, and changes to official sanctions lists.

**Potential uses include:**

* Checking whether a person, organization, company, or vessel appears in one or more of the six covered sanctions systems.
* Searching by name, alias, passport number, registration number, official sanctions ID, tax identifier, or vessel IMO number.
* Comparing how the same entity is listed by different authorities while retaining each authority’s original record and official ID.
* Reviewing designation dates, programs, aliases, identifiers, relationships, modifications, partial delistings, and full removals.
* Researching sanctions programs and the entities listed under them.
* Using country, authority, dashboard, and global-map views to identify wider geographic or program-level patterns.
* Following recent designations, modifications, and delistings through the sanctions changes and monitoring features.
* Locating the original issuing-authority record for direct verification and citation.

A typical reporting workflow is to search an entity or identifier, review the relevant entity and program pages, compare authority coverage and chronology, and then open the linked official record before publishing an important claim. Sanctions Atlas preserves authority-specific provenance and reviews imported changes before publication, but the issuing authority remains the authoritative source.

Care should be taken with common names and incomplete records. Reporters should compare dates of birth, nationalities, passport details, registration numbers, addresses, vessel identifiers, aliases, and other identifying information rather than relying on a name alone

Relationship wording should not be expanded beyond the evidence. For example, “linked to” should not be rewritten as “owned by,” “controlled by,” or “managed by” unless the official source specifically supports that conclusion.

### Summary

Sanctions Atlas is most useful for sanctions research, identity and program analysis, source verification, and monitoring changes over time. It combines detailed entity and program pages with broader analytical tools, including a dashboard, global map, authority and country views, and a frequently updated sanctions changes feed.

Its main strengths are multi-authority coverage, official-source provenance, searchable histories, and both entity-level and corpus-level analysis. It covers six authorities and is not an enterprise screening system, compliance-clearance service, or source of legal advice.

### Ethical Considerations

* Verify significant findings against the issuing authority before publication or consequential use.
* Avoid treating inclusion on a sanctions list as proof of criminal guilt beyond what the official record states.
* Distinguish clearly between active, modified, partially delisted, and fully removed records.
* Take care with common names, transliterations, aliases, and incomplete identifiers to reduce mistaken identity.
* Do not infer ownership, control, management, operation, affiliation, or personal relationships beyond the available evidence.
* Avoid unnecessarily reproducing passport numbers, identification numbers, addresses, dates of birth, or other sensitive personal information.
* Consider whether publication of personal identifiers is proportionate and in the public interest.
* Do not use Sanctions Atlas as the sole basis for denying services, closing accounts, reporting someone to authorities, or making other consequential decisions.
