### What does Open Measures do?

Open Measures is a data and analytics platform that focuses on understanding how communities and narratives form and evolve online. It collects publicly available data from across different digital spaces and structures it so researchers and journalists can analyse patterns of communication.

Instead of looking at individual posts in isolation, it helps map broader ecosystems, showing how ideas spread, which communities are connected, and where coordinated activity may be taking place.

It’s widely used in research and OSINT work focused on disinformation, extremism, and online influence operations.

**The lowdown:** It’s a strong tool for mapping how narratives and communities spread across platforms but still requires careful interpretation as it can miss context or private/closed online activity. <br>

### Cookies:&#x20;

Cookies are mainly used for analytics, A/B testing, and session management. They track site usage patterns (Google Analytics), support experimentation and feature testing, and maintain user sessions and load balancing for platform performance.

### Use in Reporting

Open Measures is useful for:

* Tracking online narratives and misinformation spread.
* Mapping extremist or niche online communities.
* Analysing discourse across platforms.
* Supporting investigations into coordinated influence campaigns.
* Providing data for research-driven journalism.

### Summary

After initial data collection, Open Measures sits in the analysis stage of the OSINT workflow, used to map how content, communities, and narratives spread across platforms, helping analysts move from individual posts to broader patterns of influence and coordination.

### Ownership

Open Measures is a privately operated research organisation based in Brooklyn, New York, founded by a small team and headed up by CEO, [Hank Teran.](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hank-teran-b3871b82/)

### Ethical Considerations

* Data is publicly sourced but should be interpreted carefully.
* Avoid mislabelling communities based on partial data.
* Be cautious when analysing sensitive or marginalised groups.
* Ensure findings are contextualised before publication.
* Respect platform terms and data use policies.
