### What does TweeterID do?

TweeterID simply converts between an X/Twitter account's unique ID and its publicly visible username/@handle. X/Twitter IDs are unique to individual accounts and, unlike usernames, do not change when an account changes its handle.

**The lowdown:** It's a lightweight identity-resolution and account-verification utility for X/Twitter, particularly useful when you need to establish the relationship between a username and its underlying account ID.

### Use in Reporting

TweeterID can be used to:

* Help establish continuity when an account's username has changed.
* Record a more persistent identifier for an X/Twitter account.
* Support identity resolution when investigating social-media accounts.
* Cross-reference an account ID with other X/Twitter evidence.
* Provide a reproducible identifier when documenting an account in an investigation.
* Support verification of whether different usernames relate to the same underlying X/Twitter account.

**Reporting Caveat:** The platform should be used alongside screenshots, archived material, and other independent evidence rather than treated as proof of the account holder's real-world identity.&#x20;

| Capabilities                                     | Limitations                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Converts X/Twitter handles to account IDs.       | Does not establish the real-world identity of an account holder.                                  |
| Converts account IDs back to usernames.          | Depends on the availability/recognition of the relevant X/Twitter account information.            |
| Provides a persistent account identifier.        | An account ID alone provides little contextual information.                                       |
| Useful for documenting social-media accounts.    | Does not independently verify that an account is authentic or controlled by a particular person.  |
| Can support account-change/continuity research.  | <p><br></p>                                                                                       |

### Summary

TweeterID is a simple, free X/Twitter account-ID conversion tool that best fits in the verification/identification stage of the OSINT workflow. Its main value is that an account ID is more persistent than an X/Twitter username, which can change over time.

### Ownership

Not clearly disclosed. Historical website metadata identifies Jared Feldman and Dillon Hess as the authors of TweeterID, while domain-registration records have used privacy protection.

### Ethical Considerations

* Treat an account ID as an identifier, not proof of a person's real-world identity.
* Be aware that usernames can change and avoid assuming that a historical username necessarily represents the same person without corroboration.
* Cross-check important findings using independent sources.
* Avoid unnecessarily collecting or publishing personal information.
* Preserve relevant evidence, including the date and time an account/handle was observed.
* Clearly distinguish between an X/Twitter account identity and the identity of the person operating the account.
