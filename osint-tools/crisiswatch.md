### What does CrisisWatch do?

CrisisWatch is designed to help users understand how political and security situations are evolving around the world. It summarises significant developments, provides qualitative assessments of the direction of a conflict or crisis, and draws attention to situations that may be deteriorating or presenting opportunities for peace.

Its principal components include a global overview, interactive map for navigating monitored conflicts and crises, individual entries, a searchable database dating back to September 2003, conflict risk alerts, indicators of developments that may provide opportunities for peace, a PDF archive, and email updates and RSS.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** Its value lies in combining regional knowledge, field reporting and expert analysis into concise assessments that help readers identify important changes across multiple conflicts.

### Use in Reporting

CrisisWatch can be used to:

* Establish the recent political and security context for a country or conflict.
* Identify situations assessed by the International Crisis Group as deteriorating, improving or remaining broadly unchanged.
* Track monthly changes in conflict dynamics using the searchable historical database.
* Identify risks of escalation and opportunities for peace highlighted by analysts.
* Research developments that may be underreported in mainstream news coverage.
* Build a chronology of political, diplomatic and security developments across multiple months.
* Identify relevant International Crisis Group reports and briefings for further investigation.
* Compare historical assessments with subsequent developments to examine how a situation evolved.
* Provide expert-analysis context alongside quantitative conflict-event data and humanitarian indicators.
* Generate investigative leads for follow-up using official documents, local reporting, international organisations and other independent sources.

**Reporting Note:** When using CrisisWatch in a published report, identify the relevant month and distinguish the source's assessment from independently established facts. Where possible, link to the specific entry or underlying report rather than citing only the main CrisisWatch page.

### Summary

CrisisWatch is a free global conflict-monitoring and early-warning resource that provides monthly qualitative assessments, identifies potential escalation risks and opportunities for peace, and enables users to explore historical developments dating back to September 2003.

For OSINT and investigative reporting, its principal value is conflict context, trend analysis, historical comparison and lead generation. It’s particularly useful for understanding why analysts consider a situation to be changing, rather than simply counting incidents. As always, significant factual claims should still be corroborated with primary documents and other reliable sources.

### Ownership

The platform is published by the International Crisis Group (ICG), an independent organisation HQ’d in Brussels, working to prevent and resolve deadly conflict.

### Ethical Considerations

* Distinguish assessment from fact. Attribute qualitative judgements to the International Crisis Group rather than presenting them as universally agreed conclusions.
* Escalation alerts identify concerns and possibilities, not certainty about what will happen.
* Conflict dynamics are complex; a single monthly assessment cannot capture every local or regional development.
* Where sources disagree, describe their differing evidence and perspectives rather than assuming they are equally supported.
* Consider civilian safety. Avoid publishing sensitive information that could expose vulnerable people, witnesses or local sources to harm.
* Use responsibly: Employ CrisisWatch for research, analysis, public-interest reporting and conflict prevention, not as a standalone basis for targeting individuals or groups.
