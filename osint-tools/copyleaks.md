### What does Copyleaks do?

Copyleaks compares submitted text against billions of webpages, academic publications and internal databases to identify copied, paraphrased or AI-generated content. It generates similarity scores, highlights matching passages and links to potential source material where available. &#x20;

**The lowdown:** It’s most useful when verifying whether content has been copied from other sources.

### Use in Reporting

Copyleaks is useful during:

* Plagiarism and copyright infringement investigations.
* Misinformation and disinformation analysis by tracing reused or republished content.
* Content provenance investigations to identify potential original publishers or earliest known sources.
* Academic misconduct and research integrity investigations.
* Brand protection and intellectual property investigations, including unauthorised reuse of published material.
* AI-generated content assessments to support investigations involving synthetic or machine-generated text.

**Note:** Similarity scores should be treated as indicators rather than definitive evidence and should always be supported by manual review of matched sources.&#x20;

### Summary

Copyleaks is best used during the verification, analysis and reporting stages of the OSINT workflow. It helps investigators determine whether content has been copied, heavily paraphrased or is likely AI-generated, while providing potential source matches for further investigation.&#x20;

However, it should be used to support evidence gathering rather than as standalone proof, with all findings independently verified against the original sources.

### Ownership

Copyleaks is a privately held Israeli SaaS company owned and operated by its co-founders, [Alon Yamin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/alonyamin/) (CEO) and [Yehonatan Bitton](https://www.linkedin.com/in/yehonatanbitton/) (CTO).

### Ethical Considerations

* Do not upload sensitive, classified or personal information without appropriate authorisation.
* Verify all matched sources independently before drawing conclusions.
* Treat AI detection results as supporting evidence rather than definitive proof.
* Consider copyright, privacy and organisational data handling policies when submitting documents.
* Be transparent about the limitations of plagiarism and AI detection when reporting findings.
