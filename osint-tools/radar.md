### What does Radar do?

Radar continuously polls hundreds of public sources (news sites, official Telegram channels, market and sports feeds), clusters reports about the same event, auto-translates headlines and summaries into the reader's language, and shows a live timeline per topic (world, Middle East, markets, sports, trends).

Analysts can set keywords (names, places, tickers, hashtags) and get an alert the moment a matching report arrives, with a link to the original source. Public report pages (/r/...) are crawlable and can be cited directly.

**The lowdown:** It monitors public information sources and brings reports together in a single feed.

### Use in Reporting

Radar can support:

* Discovering breaking-news reports relevant to an investigation.
* Monitoring military, security and geopolitical developments.
* Following public Telegram channels alongside other news sources.
* Tracking keywords, names, organisations, locations and events.
* Finding reports in languages the researcher does not speak.
* Identifying local or regional sources for further investigation.
* Building a provisional chronology of developing events.
* Comparing how different outlets report the same incident.
* Locating original articles and public-source material for verification.
* Identifying leads for deeper geolocation, imagery, document or social-media analysis.
* Monitoring new reporting after an initial event or announcement.
* Supporting situational awareness during fast-moving investigations.

**Reporting Caveat:** Radar should be cited as a discovery or monitoring tool, while the original source should normally be cited for the underlying claim.

### Summary

Radar is a partially free, real-time news and public-source monitoring platform that aggregates reporting, translates it into five languages and allows users to follow topics or keywords through alerts.

It’s most useful at the monitoring/early-warning stage of the OSINT workflow and for finding original sources to cite. Investigators should verify important claims against original sources, corroborate them independently and preserve timestamps and URLs.

### Ownership

Built and owned by Ofir Zarfati, an independent developer based in Israel. Not affiliated with any news organisation or government. Source code conventions and changelog are public on GitHub (github.com/afi1982/News).

### Ethical Considerations

* Treat every item as a lead, not a fact: verify at the original source.
* Translations are automatic; quote the original language when it matters.
* Content comes from public channels; be mindful when amplifying unverified claims from Telegram.
* Alerts on personal names should respect privacy and local law.
