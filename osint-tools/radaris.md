### What does Radaris do?

Radaris is a people-search and public-information aggregation service that brings together information from multiple sources into searchable profiles.

It can help identify possible current and previous addresses, telephone numbers, email addresses, relatives and associates, professional/business information, property-related information, possible social media or online-profile links, or other publicly available records.

**The lowdown:** Radaris describes itself as a search engine for information about people, properties, businesses and professionals.

The site uses a combination of analytics, advertising, security and site-functionality cookies. Google Analytics cookies (\_ga and \_ga\_\*) support visitor and usage measurement; \_gcl\_au is associated with Google's advertising/tagging infrastructure; cf\_clearance is a Cloudflare security cookie used to confirm successful browser verification; and Radaris-specific cookies such as lastPageVisited support site/session functionality.&#x20;

### Use in Reporting

Radaris is most useful for identity and contact-information discovery and for generating investigative leads.

For example, an investigator may use it to:

* Locate possible current or historical addresses.
* Identify possible telephone numbers.
* Identify possible email addresses.
* Discover names of relatives or associates.
* Identify possible employment or business connections.
* Locate social-media references.
* Identify property or address associations.
* Pivot from one identifier to another.
* Compare information against independent public sources.

**Important Note:** User reports have described inaccurate associations, while Radaris itself has acknowledged that records can sometimes represent another person with the same name.

### Summary

Radaris is best positioned in the discovery and enrichment stage of the OSINT workflow and is particularly useful after initial identification. It can generate additional identifiers such as historical addresses, possible relatives, associates or contact details that can be taken into other sources.

The best investigative practice is to use Radaris to generate leads, then verify those leads against primary or authoritative sources.

### Ownership

The site is operated by two native Russian brothers living in Massachusetts. For years, Radaris used a fabricated identity and a fake person named as the CEO from its inception.

### Ethical Considerations

* Verify identity before attributing information to an individual.
* Avoid relying on a name-only match where other people have the same name.
* Avoid publishing unnecessary addresses, telephone numbers or other sensitive personal information.
* Verify consequential information before publishing or acting on it.&#x20;
* Respect applicable restrictions concerning sensitive personal information and background checks.
