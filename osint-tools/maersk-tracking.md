### What does Maersk Tracking do?

Maersk Tracking allows users to track containers, cargo, and shipments across the global logistics network by entering identifiers such as container numbers, booking references or shipment numbers.

The information available depends on the shipment and may include container status, estimated arrival dates, vessel information, port locations and transport milestones.

**The lowdown:** It provides publicly accessible shipment information that can help verify logistics activity, build timelines and identify potential links between companies, ports, airports and transport routes.

### How to Use:

**1. Access the Maersk Tracking page, select Ocean, Air, Less-than-container-load (LCL) or Parcel, then enter a known container or Bill of Lading number, Air Waybill number, booking or tracking number to search.**

### Use in Reporting

Maersk Tracking can support investigations and intelligence reporting involving both maritime and air logistics activity. It can be used to verify shipment movements, confirm delivery timelines and support supply chain analysis.

**Potential reporting uses include:**

* Tracking ocean container movements.
* Tracking air cargo shipments.
* Establishing shipment timelines.
* Verifying import/export activity.
* Supporting corporate due diligence.
* Mapping supply chain relationships.
* Investigating logistics networks.
* Supporting sanctions and trade compliance research.
* Corroborating information from shipping documents.
* Identifying links between companies, ports, airports and transport routes.

### Summary

Maersk Tracking is a valuable logistics intelligence resource for monitoring global cargo movements across ocean and air freight networks. Within the OSINT workflow, it fits best during the collection and verification phases, where known shipment identifiers can be used to confirm movements, locations and timelines. The information can then support analysis by helping investigators map supply chains, identify relationships between organisations and understand patterns of commercial activity.&#x20;

### Ethical Considerations

* Use tracking information only for legitimate research, compliance or investigative purposes.
* Verify shipping data using multiple independent sources.
* Avoid making assumptions about cargo contents based only on shipment movements.
* Consider commercial sensitivity when reporting logistics information.
* Ensure findings are supported by corroborating evidence.
