### What does Global Fishing Watch do?

Global Fishing Watch is an open-access maritime intelligence platform that enables you to monitor commercial fishing vessels, analyse historical tracks, identify fishing patterns and observe activity in territorial waters and the high seas.

Historical movement data and map layers help establish timelines, identify vessel interactions and support wider maritime intelligence assessments.

**The lowdown:** It provides valuable insight into maritime activity, allowing you to investigate illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing, sanctions evasion, smuggling routes, transshipment events, environmental crime and vessel behaviour.

### Cookies:&#x20;

The site uses Google Analytics cookies (_ga_\*) to collect anonymous usage statistics, a Google Ads attribution cookie (\_gcl\_au) to measure marketing effectiveness, and functional cookies to remember language preferences (i18next) and interface settings (panelWidths). An additional analytics cookie records the visitor's initial traffic source.&#x20;

These cookies support website performance, analytics and user experience rather than investigative functionality.

### Use in Reporting

Global Fishing Watch can be used to:

* Track fishing vessel movements over time.
* Investigate illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing activity.
* Identify potential ship-to-ship (transshipment) interactions.
* Support sanctions evasion and maritime smuggling investigations.
* Develop timelines of vessel movements and operational patterns.
* Corroborate maritime intelligence obtained from AIS databases and satellite imagery.

### Summary

Global Fishing Watch is a powerful maritime OSINT platform, best used during the geospatial analysis, movement analysis and intelligence enrichment phases of the OSINT workflow.&#x20;

**Note:** Its findings should be validated with complementary sources such as MarineTraffic, VesselFinder, sanctions databases, satellite imagery and official vessel registries.

### Ownership

Independent international nonprofit organization, originally founded in 2015 as a collaborative project by 3 partner organizations: Oceana, SkyTruth, and Google. [Tony Long](https://globalfishingwatch.org/tony-long/) is the current CEO.

### Ethical Considerations

* AIS data may be incomplete, delayed or intentionally manipulated.
* Machine-learning classifications should not be treated as conclusive evidence.
* Consider legitimate operational reasons for unusual vessel behaviour.
* Respect licensing and data usage restrictions when reproducing maps or datasets.
