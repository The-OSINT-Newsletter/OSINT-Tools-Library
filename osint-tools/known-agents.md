### What does Known Agents do?

Known Agents helps website owners see and manage how AI systems and bots interact with their sites. Instead of focusing on human identity, it focuses on non-human traffic intelligence, giving visibility into scraping, crawling, and automated access patterns.

**The lowdown:** It’s primarily a web intelligence and monitoring tool rather than a traditional OSINT identity platform.

### Cookies:&#x20;

The site uses a fairly large amount of Google authentication, security, and localisation cookies to manage login sessions, verify identity, prevent abuse, and personalise search results (including location-based data and safety controls).

### Use in Reporting

Known Agents can support reporting or technical investigations by:

* Identifying automated or AI-driven traffic to websites.
* Helping distinguish human vs bot interaction patterns.
* Providing context on scraping or crawler activity.
* Supporting infrastructure and web security analysis.
* Assisting in understanding AI indexing behaviour on sites.

In general reporting terms, Known Agents is used to provide context on AI and bot activity targeting a website, helping explain automated traffic, scraping behaviour, and crawler interactions rather than attributing human identity.&#x20;

### Summary

Known Agents provides visibility into automated traffic and allows site owners to control how non-human systems interact with their content. It’s not typically used for identity attribution or profiling, but more for understanding machine-driven activity around a target domain.

### Ethical Considerations

* Use Google authentication and session cookies only with appropriate consent and legitimate access.
* Comply with applicable privacy and data protection laws.
* Avoid using cookies or session identifiers for identifying, tracking, or profiling individuals without a lawful and proportionate basis.
* Treat authentication and session cookies as sensitive data and avoid exposing them in reports, logs, or shared materials.
* Focus analysis on security, technical, or system behaviour rather than attempts to de-anonymise users or attribute real-world identity.
