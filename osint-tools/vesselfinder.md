### What does VesselFinder do?

VesselFinder provides a searchable map and database of vessels using AIS (Automatic Identification System) transmissions.&#x20;

Depending on the access level, information can include:

* Current or most recently received vessel positions.
* Vessel name, IMO number and MMSI number.
* Vessel type, flag, dimensions and other technical particulars.
* Speed, course, heading and navigational status.
* Reported destination and estimated time of arrival (ETA).
* Previous ports of call and voyage information.
* Historical AIS positions and vessel movement tracks.
* Port arrivals, departures and other recorded events.

The platform also provides commercial AIS data services and an API for retrieving structured vessel, voyage, port-call and ship-particulars information.

**The lowdown:** It allows users to follow the reported movements of ships and investigate their connections with ports, terminals, and geographical locations.

### Use in Reporting

VesselFinder is useful for:

* Establishing the reported location of a vessel at a particular time using available AIS position records.
* Reconstructing a vessel's reported route and identifying previous port calls.
* Investigating shipping activity involving particular ports, terminals, anchorages or geographical areas.
* Identifying vessels that may have visited locations relevant to an investigation.
* Examining unusual or unexpected changes in a vessel's reported route, destination, speed or navigational status.
* Comparing the movements of multiple vessels to identify possible connections or patterns requiring further investigation.
* Researching vessels potentially connected to sanctions investigations, illegal fishing, forced labour or the movement of restricted goods.
* Establishing a timeline of vessel movements to compare with news reports, satellite imagery, port records and other open-source information.

As an example, [Bellingcat discusses using VesselFinder](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/case-studies/2015/08/19/yachtspotting/) in this article to examine the movements of the yacht Maltese Falcon across the Mediterranean as part of an investigation into the luxury yachts associated with people connected to the Russian political elite.&#x20;

### Summary

VesselFinder’s combination of a public vessel-tracking map, historical AIS information, port-call records and commercial data services makes it useful for maritime research and investigative reporting. In particular, it can help identify patterns in shipping activity, establish movement timelines and generate leads for investigations involving sanctions, illegal fishing, forced labour and other maritime issues.

However, its findings should always be corroborated with independent sources, such as port records, satellite imagery, official maritime information, company records and reliable reporting. Remember AIS data is not conclusive proof of a vessel's physical location or activity.

### Ethical Considerations

* AIS positions are reported data and should not automatically be treated as proof of a vessel's actual physical location.
* An interruption in AIS transmissions does not, by itself, prove deliberate concealment or suspicious activity.
* AIS data can be inaccurate, delayed, incomplete or manipulated. Corroborate significant findings with independent evidence.
* A vessel's visit to a port does not prove that it loaded or unloaded a particular cargo.
* Vessel names, flags, registered owners and managers can change. Use IMO numbers where possible to distinguish vessels and check identity changes.
* Avoid attributing a vessel's actions directly to its registered owner, manager, crew or beneficial owner without supporting evidence.
* Be careful when publishing the movements of vessels where disclosure could create safety or security risks.
