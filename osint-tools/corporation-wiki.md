### What does Corporation Wiki do?

Corporation Wiki pulls together publicly available corporate records and turns them into searchable profiles. Instead of digging through multiple state registries, it gives you a single view of companies, directors, and the relationships between them.

For OSINT work, it’s especially helpful when you’re trying to understand who is connected to whom in a corporate structure, or when tracking individuals across multiple business entities.

**The lowdown:** It helps users explore corporate structures, ownership links, and historical company filings.

### Use in Reporting

Corporation Wiki can support:

* Corporate due diligence investigations.
* Director and officer profiling.
* Business relationship mapping.
* Shell company identification (supporting analysis).
* Background checks on corporate entities.
* Network analysis of business structures

It’s particularly useful for identifying recurring individuals across multiple companies.

### Summary

Corporation Wiki is best suited to the discovery, enrichment and link analysis stages of the OSINT workflow, helping analysts map corporate networks and identify connections between entities before deeper verification and reporting.

It is widely used for identifying corporate relationships, tracking director activity, and supporting business intelligence investigations.

### Ownership

Operated by [Corporation Wiki LLC](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/corporation-wiki), a private company specialising in corporate data aggregation and business intelligence services, based in Winter Park, Florida. No further ownership details are disclosed.

### Ethical Considerations

* Always verify corporate data against official registry sources.
* Be cautious of outdated or incorrectly linked entities.
* Avoid over-reliance on aggregated data for legal or financial conclusions.
* Respect privacy when analysing individual directors or officers.
* Ensure compliance with data protection and legitimate interest requirements.
* Use as a supporting tool rather than a primary evidential source.
