### What does UCDP do?

UCDP provides a systematic, research-based record of organised violence around the world. Its data is designed to help users understand where and when violent events occurred, which organised actors were involved, how conflicts developed and how levels of violence changed over time.

UCDP has collected conflict data since 1979 and has developed definitions and coding procedures that allow researchers to compare conflict patterns across countries and historical periods. Its data is widely used in peace and conflict research and by practitioners and policymakers.&#x20;

**The lowdown:** It’s essentially a global organised-violence research database.

### Use in Reporting

UCDP can be used to:

* Identify recorded organised-violence events associated with a particular country, region or conflict.
* Establish a structured starting point for researching a reported attack or violent incident.
* Examine how conflict events are distributed geographically and how recorded activity changes over time.
* Investigate the actors identified in UCDP records and examine their involvement in different conflicts.
* Compare patterns of state-based conflict, non-state conflict and one-sided violence against civilians.
* Analyse recorded battle-related deaths and compare estimates across conflict years or opposing parties.
* Identify changes in conflict activity that may warrant further investigation.
* Examine whether a reported incident is consistent with the dates, locations and actors recorded in an existing dataset.
* Generate leads for further research using local news, international reporting, humanitarian publications, official statements and other open sources.
* Produce maps, timelines, tables and statistical summaries of documented organised violence.
* Support comparative research across countries, conflicts and historical periods.
* Provide contextual data for reporting on armed conflict, civilian harm, conflict escalation and peace processes.

[World Bank research](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/449471635478676087/pdf/The-Risk-That-Travels-with-You-Links-between-Forced-Displacement-Conflict-and-Intimate-Partner-Violence-in-Colombia-and-Liberia.pdf) used UCDP conflict data to examine the relationship between exposure to armed conflict, forced displacement and intimate partner violence. The report describes how the georeferenced dataset records state-based conflict, non-state conflict and one-sided violence, and uses historical conflict records to study Colombia and Liberia.

### Summary

UCDP is a valuable OSINT and research resource for analysing organised violence, armed conflict, conflict actors, event locations and reported fatalities. Its structured datasets can help identify patterns and compare conflict activity across time and geography.

For investigative reporting, the platform is particularly useful for building a conflict timeline, mapping recorded events, identifying actors for further research and providing quantitative context. However, it should be used as a structured research source rather than a complete or real-time register of all violent incidents.

### Ownership

[UCDP](https://www.uu.se/en/department/peace-and-conflict-research/research/ucdp/about-ucdp) operates within the Department of Peace and Conflict Research at Uppsala University, Sweden. It’s an academic research programme with a long-running focus on the systematic collection and analysis of organised-violence data.

UCDP is also part of [demscore](https://www.demscore.se/); an e-infrastructure supporting access to harmonised research datasets. UCDP identifies Swedish Research Council national research infrastructure grant 2021-00162 as a source of funding.

### Ethical Considerations

* Distinguish battle-related deaths from broader conflict-related deaths, including indirect deaths caused by displacement, hunger, disease or the breakdown of healthcare.
* Treat UCDP casualty figures as estimates based on available reporting and coding, rather than definitive counts of every person killed.
* Do not infer responsibility for an attack, intent, criminal conduct or individual culpability from UCDP data alone.
* Avoid treating the absence of a recorded event as proof that no violence occurred; underreporting, limited access and incomplete information may affect coverage.
* Distinguish direct combat deaths from deaths caused by one-sided violence against civilians and other forms of organised violence, using the appropriate UCDP dataset and definitions.
* Check UCDP's data-use terms and attribution requirements before reproducing datasets, maps, statistics or visualisations in a report.&#x20;
